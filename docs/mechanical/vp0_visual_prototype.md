## 2. Architecture (v0.17)

The **cradle** holds the head. On each side a **Nexus** sits on the disk axis between the cradle's hub node and the disk: it mounts the disk and carries the visor pivot. The **disks** are sealed lenses hung at their centre. Nothing else touches the disks. Every adjustable joint is held by a captive pin or a screw, never by friction, and nothing that comes out of the helm is a loose part.

**Cradle.** A 30 × 7 mm PETG band on the hat line (z 40..70 at the sides, above the ears; its lower edge rises 12 mm over the forehead so the pad sits above the eyebrows, the top edge stays planar for the inverted print). Its outer face carries the **spine**: a 4 × 4 channel at z 63..67 on every free span between the nodes and around the front, for a 2 mm rope potted in metal-filled epoxy under a flush 1.5 mm cover strip glued in wet. The epoxy alone is weaker than the plastic; the rope carries tension across a cracked band and the epoxy bonds the three into one. The rear halves carry the same channel, 3 deep. Nodes are solid blocks; each span is bonded over its length, no anchors needed. one U print for the forehead and sides with two solid nodes per side and a 3 mm doubler on the outer face for 30 mm either side of the hub node (a disk hit puts about 6 N·m into that node; the doubler takes the band from 48 to 24 MPa), plus two tilted rear halves that meet in the nape module. 10 mm foam blocks velcro to the inside (forehead, sides; 6 mm under the nodes); the crown pad sits under the arch apex. Cables run on the inside of the band under the foam. Strap slot pairs behind the hub nodes and on the rear halves take a Y chin strap if the shake test asks for one. **v0.13**: a 3 × 3.4 mm **cable channel in the band's bottom face** runs from ±27 mm of arc at the brow (under the sensor bar's exit slots) around each front corner to x 36, where a 4 mm groove with an 8 × 8 mouth climbs the outer face into the LED bore (36, 55). It is on the shared cradle print (invisible from outside, an open groove on the inverted print) and carries the combat trim's bar cables inside under the foam; the full trim does not use it.

| Node | x | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| hub | −10..30, on the disk axis, z 43..70 | three M3 × 30 through-holes for the Nexus with hex nut pockets on the inner face; 8.3 socket from the top (crown arch coupler); Ø5 coil-cable bore at x −5, z 59.5 in line with the flange's |
| rear | −58..−32, hangs 18 below the band | one-sided tapered tenon socket for the rear half (nail); 8.3 socket from the top (antenna pylon) |

**Cylinder port standard.** 8 mm peg, 8.3 mm socket, 15 mm deep, M3 cross-bolt (or a nail) at 7 mm from the mouth. Used at four places: two arch couplers, two pylon pegs. Printed pegs take a nail down their core with epoxy; 8 mm aluminium rod drilled with the guide is the hit-rated option. Spares: double-male coupler, drill guide. No unused sockets, so no plugs.

**Nexus (PETG).** Inboard to outboard on the disk axis: the hub node's outer face (y 100); the **spool**: a Ø44 × 1 thrust plate with a Ø36 × 8 hub bearing on the flange's flat inner face (the visor ring's bearing and the axis bolt's spacer, with pawl notches at the six visor positions at y 103..107, the pawl's own y range) and a web rising to a **spacer block** (x ±10, z 58..68.5) that the two upper bolts pass through; the **visor ring** (Ø44/36.3 × 7, the root of each brow rail); a wave washer; the **flange**, Ø68 × 8 at y 109..117, carrying the **hollow** Ø20 bayonet stalk (Ø10 bore, 1.2 mm root fillet, lugs, a radial Ø3.4 lock-pin hole at the top), the Ø5 coil-cable bore, a lanyard hole up-back. Three M3 × 30 go in from OUTSIDE with heads in counterbores under the disk plate, through flange, hub or spacer and node, to nuts in the node's inner-face pockets. No ear, no lock screw. L and R flanges are mirrored STLs.

**Visor lock: the pawl slider.** A 2.6 × 3 PETG bar in a tunnel along each rail's axis, inserted from the ring's bore. Its 2.4 mm tip drops into one of the hub's notches (−17, −2, 13, 28, 43, 58°); a Ø3 × 10 spring on its stem seats against a step at r 36; its thumb tab pokes out of the rail's top edge at r 38..41, just ahead of the flange rim. Thumb on the tab, pull 2 mm, swing, let go: 15° steps, one hand. The tip is the visor's fuse and the slider is a two-minute print. The flat-flexure variant replaces the spring if none is small enough.

**Disks.** A 2.0 mm paraboloid dome shell (outside) on a 3 mm rim wall with six screw bosses, closed by a 1.6 mm back plate on six M3. The back plate **seats on the nexus flange face**, so a rim hit reacts over a 34 mm lever instead of the bore. The plate's cavity side carries the **bayonet** (Ø20.6 bore with two entry notches, a groove the 3 mm lugs turn 90° into whose floor **cams** from 4.5 to 3.8 mm over the turn, so the plate is pulled 0.2 mm onto the flange face and cannot rattle, and a stop wall) and a **lock notch** in the boss bore 98° past the stalk pin. The **centre knob** (Ø28, flush in a 5 mm dish at the dome apex, PETG) carries a Ø7.6 shaft down the axis through the coil's free centre into the stalk's bore; its eccentric end (a Ø6 cam offset 1 mm, guided by the stalk's 8.2 mm bore) pushes a 7.8 mm piece of 3.2 nail out of the stalk 1.8 mm into that notch; the nail's outer end is filed round so the notch wall pushes it back in when the cam is low. Turn the disk to the stop (about 107°: the 6 mm lugs span 30° of the groove and the stop bumps 14°, and the flat, fully-cammed part of the floor now runs to 135° so the whole lug sits on it), half a turn on the knob: the disk cannot rotate or lift. Half a turn back releases. The plate's coil-lead exit is a 5 mm **arc slot** at the bore radius spanning that travel: the lead threaded through the flange bore is never dragged, the plate turns around it, and a disk comes off with its lead attached on 20 mm of slack at the hub connector. The former's exit grooves and the dome's unlock notch are placed for the locked pose; the assembly shows the disks locked. A C-clip on the shaft inside the dome's apex boss keeps the knob in the disk. The coil feed leaves through a Ø5 hole at x −5, z 59.5, straight into the flange's bore. Nothing protrudes from the plate's head face, so it prints flat. The dome prints **on edge** (axis horizontal) on a 0.15 mm flat at the rim, the only orientation in which a 10 mm sag shell is self-supporting; a notch in the dish rim at the bottom marks the knob's unlocked position. No metal on the disk axis (the knob shaft is PETG; the lock pin is 8 mm off it). The stalk is the fuse: a 100 N rim hit puts about 6 N·m on it, under 10 MPa.

- **Crown arch**: two identical chamfered 20 × 7 halves ending in a foot block with an 8.3 socket over the hub node's socket; a 30 mm double-male coupler (M3 cross-bolt each end) joins them. Straight up 20 mm then the superellipse to the overlap bar in the apex sleeve (ridges, M4 clamp, ±13 mm of span). Two cross-bolts out and it lifts off. No spine groove (the rope spine goes back on if a PLA arch cracks).
- **Brow**: 160 × 50 × 15 centre panel with a 116 × 32 LED bay opening to the FRONT under a **faceplate** (four M3 × 8 into the top and bottom walls), a rounded top bead and a Ø4 cable hole from each link pocket into the bay. Two **rails** (7 × 20, 26 mm-tall root gusset, ending at s 90) whose roots are the visor rings on the nexus spools: the visor pivots about the disk centre. Each rail ends in a half-lap where a **link** takes over: a 3.5 mm lap on the rail's inner half (two M3 × 10 from the rail's outer face), then a 7 × 20 bar bending 53° inboard behind the panel into a 10 mm pocket in the panel's back face (one M3 × 16 from the underside, plus a 1.5 mm mouth relief). From the front only the panel shows. Reach = link length; swap the front module for anything with two pockets 160 mm apart. The LED cable runs in a 2.5 mm groove along the rail's and link's undersides. Links print standing with the bend in-plane.
- **Antenna pylons**: 8 mm peg base in each rear node socket with a clevis; a faceted 140 mm blade (6 mm serrated root tongue, flares to 28 × 14, tapers to 12 × 6; hollow with 2.4 mm walls, Ø4 wire bore) on an M3 hinge with a wave washer and a Ø24 printed knob: 15° notches, hand-locked, folds back along the rear half. Deployed 30° back from vertical.
- **Nape, print one: the pin-lock.** A 60 × 44 × 10.6 block with two rack tunnels (each open at its own end) and a four-lobe slot of Ø2.2 holes at u = 0, p/2, p, 3p/2 on the mid-plane. Both rear-half racks (54 mm) slide through; two 2 mm nails dropped through the top wall pass both racks' tooth spaces and the web between them, two teeth per rack. Whole-pitch settings use the 0 and p holes, half-pitch ones p/2 and 3p/2: 3.9 mm steps of circumference, ±14 mm of range. Heads sit flush in counterbores, the bottom wall is a snug 2.1, a lanyard hole takes the thread. Prints standing so the holes are round. The enclosed **pull dial** (52 × 44 × 26.5: body with ratchet ring, cover, lid, dial, key cap, pinion, retainer with an M5 nut pocket) stays in the generator as the second module (`NAPE_MODULE = "dial"`) for when one-handed adjustment matters.

Overall width at the knobs: 287 mm. Disk back face 40 mm off the skull.

### Pad frames (v0.12, both trims)

The plain foam blocks at the forehead and under the nodes become **printed frames** that the foam glues to, so the contactless-or-dry sensors of Track M sit where the plan puts them and stay put under sweat and movement. Every frame is a 2 mm plate 0.1 mm off the cradle's inner face on two M3 × 8 thread-forming screws from the head side (heads under the foam, 2.5 mm taps in the cradle); the foam goes over the plate, cut around the pillars.

- **Forehead** (`pad_front`): a curved plate along the band's inner front face, ±45 mm of arc, z 52.2..69.8, with three windows and screws at ±36. Three flat **carriers** plug into the windows from the head side (a 2 mm plug fills the window, a 1.4 mm flange sits on the plate, epoxied): the centre one carries a 21.6 × 15 × 4 pocket for the MAX30102 module, the two at ±25 a 12 × 13 × 0.8 recess for a conductive-fabric EDA patch. Their towers stop 1 mm under the 8 mm foam's surface, so the foam compresses onto the sensors instead of the sensors standing proud. Flat carriers on a ~73 mm arc converge inboard, hence the 4 mm gap at the plate. Cables leave through a notch in each tower's bottom wall and run under the foam.
- **Hub** (`pad_hub_L/R`, mirrored): 38 × 25 on the node's inner face with a Ø17.6 pillar (Ø15 × 2 recess) at (19, 54.5) for the bone-conduction transducer, a Ø5.4 hole in line with the coil bore and a Ø6.5 clearance over the hub socket's cross-bolt so the crown coupler can still be pinned with the plate on. The nexus nuts sit in their pockets under the plate.
- **Rear** (`pad_rear_L/R`, mirrored): 24 × 24 with a 13 × 10 × 2.5 pocket pillar for the MAX30205 (occipital skin, out of the sweat line of the forehead) and the same cross-bolt clearance. The temperature sensor moved here from the hub because the hub plate could not hold the transducer seat, the cross-bolt clearance and a pocket at the same time.

### Electronics core and harness (v0.15)

The Stabilizer as derived is a resonance-breathing pacer with closed-loop regulation and the coil as an experimental arm. What it needs on the head is the sensor set already carried by the pad frames and the sensor bar, two bone-conduction transducers in the hub seats, an LED lane, and a host with a battery. Where the host and battery live is a choice you can make per session:

| `--core` | On the helm | Off the helm | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `none` | nape core base + cap | belt pack with the host, a big pack and the coil driver; umbilical into the base | belt-only |
| `nape` | base + nape pod (Heltec, 1000 mAh LiPo, IMU, GSR module, amp) | optional belt pack for the coil driver, plugged into the base | helm-only or both |
| `slab` | base + cap, two slab pods on the rear-node sockets (compute in one, power in the other) | optional | the socket option; replaces the pylons |

**Nape core base** (`nape_core_base`, module `core`): the pin-lock block with a 12 mm junction bay behind it. Every helm cable comes through two Ø4 holes in the block's web (between the rack tunnels, under the nape pad) onto a 30 × 20 junction board on two posts. The bay's bottom wall has a 16 × 10 connector window for the umbilical with zip-tie slots either side, its outer wall a 16 × 8 jumper window and two M3 bosses that take either the cap or the nape pod. It prints standing like the pin-lock.

**Nape pod** (`core_pod_nape` + lid, 62 × 54 × 24): the LiPo lies on the floor, the Heltec on it with foam between, the small boards in the 16 mm strip beside them. OLED window in the lid, USB-C window in the +u end for charging, three 12 mm tact switches in the top wall under printed caps (the big middle cap is the tally: tap the back of your head; the outer two are round and mode; session start and end is a long press of mode), a slide-switch slot in the other end. About 85 g of pod and contents at x −112 cancels the full trim's nose-down moment.

**Slab pods** (`core_pod_slab` + lid, 62 × 22 × 40) stand on a rear-node socket via `core_socket_foot` (the port standard's 8 mm peg with an M3 cross-pin). A flat pod there would reach 26 mm into the head, so the slab is thin across and tall: the Heltec stands on edge in one, the LiPo and small boards in the other. OLED window and buttons face outboard. They sit well outside the headgear rule, like the nodes they stand on.

**Belt pack** (`belt_pack` + lid, 105 × 70 × 40): four corner bosses, two belt slots, an Ø8 umbilical hole with zip-tie slots, a USB window. Holds the Heltec for belt-only sessions, a 2 × 18650 holder, the coil driver and a buck converter. The umbilical is an 8-way lead: coil drive for two disks, aux power, a data pair.

**Build policy for one or two helms (v0.16).** The host always rides the helm next to its sensors: a metre of umbilical is too long for the 400 kHz I2C bus and the GSR module's analogue line. The belt pack is power, the coil driver and the log sink (the firmware streams NDJSON over USB only; a Pi Zero on the umbilical's USB records it and carries the serial keys until the pod has buttons). So the nape pod is the standard core, `--core none` is the base with the cap and an umbilical to a belt pack that carries a host of its own, and the slab pods are held for the combat balance test. Combat sessions do not use the nape pod: with no crown arch, 85 g at the nape lifts the forehead off the pads and the bar off its aim. Build one is the full trim on an open head, disks last; build two, if there is one, is the combat cradle variant with slim nodes and a flush nape lock (M-M6), which is what the headgear rule has been asking for since v0.12.

**Coil spool (v0.17)** (`coil_former`, two prints per disk): the spiral former is replaced by a single flange, Ø109.4 × 1.8, with a Ø40/44 × 3 hub ring, six boss holes, a 3.5 × 8 × 3 solder-pad boss outside the winding (inside the Ø110 cavity wall) and a Ø3 lead hole over the plate's arc slot; a 1.4 × 0.55 groove on the back face (the bed face) runs from the pad's tie hole to the lead hole for the winding's end pair (24 AWG enamelled is 0.56), leaving a 1.25 mm floor (the 1.2 flange of the plan left 0.2 mm under a 1.0 groove). The bifilar pair is scramble-wound around the hub onto the flange, tacked with a drop of glue every few turns, ends to the pad; a second identical spool stacks on the first, its flange resting on the hub and the winding, and carries the counter-wound sham layer. A 5 mm foam ring above the stack (two spools: 9.6 mm) holds it against the dome. The cavity keeps its 12 mm: the bayonet boss and the apex boss meet in it. Foil lining on the dome's interior and the plate's cavity face gives the sanctuary shield element; the first spool stands 0.2 mm off the plate for it. The lead carries eight conductors to an 8-pin connector behind the hub plate.

**Bay coil** (`bay_spool`): a 100 × 25 × 1.2 flange with a 70 × 8 × 3 rounded core in the visor bay, flange against the bay's back wall behind the LED strip. With the disk pair it makes a two-axis rotating field at the brain's centre; its lead follows the LED cable through the link and rail grooves.

**Coil former (v0.15, kept as `style = "spiral"`)** (`coil_former`, one print per disk): a Ø109.4 × 3.5 disc on the back plate's cavity side, captured by the six dome bosses through its holes and cleared by the bayonet boss through its Ø40 centre. One spiral furrow on the outer face takes the bifilar pair side by side from r 21 to r 50 (about 15 turn-pairs at 1.9 mm pitch, a little under the derivation's 16.8 because the outer turns would clip the boss holes); both ends drop through pin holes to grooves on the back face that end over the plate's cable hole, so all four wires leave through the existing bore with no hole through the windings. An arrow on the former clocks it to the plate's hole.

**Harness path.** Sensor bar and pads: through the bottom-face channel and LED bore, inside under the foam to the rear node, then a 3 × 2.5 groove across the node's rear face (z 29 on the left, z 48 on the right: the right rear half is the left print flipped over) onto a channel of the same size on the rear half's inner face at v −13, below the strap slots and clear of the rack, covered by a 1 mm strip (`cover_rear_in`), to 8 mm short of the rack; the last span crosses under the nape pad into the base's web holes. Coil leads take the same path in reverse from the base to the hub nodes and up through the bores. A connector in the foam layer behind each hub plate with about 20 mm of slack lets a disk come off with its lead attached.

### Combat trim (v0.12)

`assemble_vp0.py --trim combat` builds the cradle U, rear halves, nape pin-lock, spine covers and pad frames, then adds the **sensor bar** and four flush port plugs, and drops everything that hangs off the nexus. Same STLs for the shared parts.

- **Sensor bar** (`sensor_bar`, PLA): a hollow curved bar, 24 × 20 section with 1 mm chamfers, along the band's outer front face ±50 mm of arc at z 48..68, 0.3 mm off the spine cover. The 4 mm floor carries a 36 × 10 window with a 1.5 mm flush recess for the **carrier** (a curved 50 × 12 × 1.5 plate with the Ø9.6 MLX90614 hole at −11, the 8.6 mm camera square at 0 and two Ø5.2 IR LED holes at 8.5 and 15; two M3 × 8 from below into taps that break into the cavity), two Ø4 cable holes at ±30 and a 5.3 × 2.2 LED lane along the front edge for the pacer strip. The back wall takes two printed **Ø4 pins** at ±42 (10 mm long, 5 in the band's outer face: the sparring fuse, they shear before the neck does) and one M3 × 8 at the centre, driven through the window before the carrier goes in. Cables drop through two Ø3.5 slots in the back wall's foot at ±30 mm of arc, pass under the band's bottom edge into the bottom-face cable channel, follow it around the corner to x 36, climb the groove into the LED bore and run inside along the foam layer, under the hub pad's transducer pillar, to the rear node and on to the nape with the coil feed. The combat assembly carries this route as a check-only object (`cable_bar.L/R`, Ø2.5) and the collision report holds it to the channel.
- **Plugs**: the four empty sockets (crown couplers, pylons) take `port_plug_flush` (1.3 mm cap, nail through the cross hole).
- **Fit report** (`renders_combat/fit.txt`): a headgear phantom (the head phantom grown 25 mm, cut below z −10, face open to z 70 and ±55) and the sparring rule that nothing but the bar sits more than 20 mm off the skull. **Result on v0.12: the sensing parts pass (pads and carriers 14–17 mm), the bar is the allowed exception, but the v0.11 cradle itself fails**: the hub nodes are 16 mm thick on a band already 13.5 mm proud and their top-front corners sit 34 mm off the receding forehead, the band's top edge on the sides is 24 mm off the narrowing skull, and the nape pin-lock stands 37 mm proud at the occiput. A 25 mm-padded headgear will not close over it. Combat trim is therefore a mechanical prototype of the sensing parts on the shared cradle; a **combat cradle variant** (slim nodes without the nexus bolt group, a lower or chamfered top edge, a flush nape lock, or a headgear with a taller face opening) is the next mechanical decision, not something to tune by hand.

## 6. Print and validation order

1. Fit coupons A and B. On A: a 3.2 nail must press into the 3.2 hole and slip through the 3.4; a 2 mm nail through the 2.2; an M3 must self-tap into the 2.5. The 8 mm peg must slide into the socket and take the cross-bolt. The bayonet stub must drop into the boss and turn a quarter to the stop without force. The ratchet disc must click one way and lock the other. On B: the M3 nut must press into the sideways pocket, a 3.4 nail must pass the teardrop 3.4, the ring must turn on the hub, the 7.8 mm nail piece must move in the stalk hole, and a printed Ø4 pin must be snug in 4.1 and free in 4.3. Then a 3.5 mm drill through every horizontal clearance hole on the cradle before its first bolt.
2. Cradle U (PETG), both rear halves, pin-lock, pad frames, in this order. (a) Rear halves: tenons into the rear nodes, 3.2 nails through node and tenon from the inner face, heads flush in their counterbores. (b) Drop the three M3 nuts into each hub node's inner-face pockets and the cross-pin nuts (or nails) into the socket cross holes. (c) Pad frames: carriers epoxied into the front plate's windows on the bench; then the hub, rear and front plates go on with countersunk M3 × 8 from the head side. The hub plate captures the nexus nuts and the Ø5 recesses on its band face clear the bolt tips; the rear plate's edge crosses the tenon nail's counterbore, which is why the nail went in first. (d) Foam glued over the plates, cut around the towers and pillars. (e) Racks through the pin-lock, two nails in the hole pair whose spaces line up. Wear the cradle alone: it must sit above the ears (the phantom says 4 mm), stay put when you shake your head, and go on and off cap-style without opening the pin-lock. This is the chin-strap decision and the dial-versus-pin-lock decision.
3. Both nexus sets and one disk. The rails are captive once the flange is bolted, so each rail is finished first: pawl slider with its spring into the tunnel from the bore end. Spool with the rail's ring on its hub against the node, wave washer, flange, three M3 × 30 from outside into the captured nuts (thread-lock). Grease the 7.8 mm lock pin and drop it into the stalk's top hole. In the dome: knob through the apex hole from outside, C-clip on from inside, the coil apparatus (its centre hole must clear the shaft and clip), then the back plate on six M3 × 10 with the coil lead already through the plate's cable hole and about 45 mm of slack inside, because the hole swings a quarter turn on a 28 mm radius while the disk goes on. Feed the coil lead's eight wires through the flange and node bores and the hub plate's hole into the foam layer first, with the disk held at the entry angle; knob index mark at the dish notch (unlocked), lugs into the entry notches, turn to the stop (about 107°: the cam tightens over the last third), half a turn on the knob (mark up = locked). The lead stays put in the bore; the plate's arc slot turns around it. Work the visor tab through all six positions; check the ear clearance and shim the ring stack if it binds.
4. Brow centre, links, faceplate. Links into the panel's back pockets (one screw each from below), rails lapped onto the links (two screws each). Thumb the tabs, swing, let go.
5. Crown halves, couplers, apex. Couplers into the hub sockets, feet on, four cross-bolts, set the span.
6. Pylons. Second disk. The crown arch is standard for the full trim: at a kilogram, the helm needs its vertical support on a run. Then the spine, last of all: mask every socket mouth, tap hole and bore, rope in the channels, epoxy, covers pressed in wet, and pot the pegs while the epoxy is out. Wear it, run.
7. Electronics (any trim). Junction board onto the base's posts with the helm harness fed through the web holes from the head side before the nape pad goes on. Then one of: cap on (belt-only, umbilical through the bottom window with a zip tie inside); nape pod on the two bosses with its jumper through the windows, lid last; or the socket feet pinned into the rear sockets and the slabs screwed on from inside. Coil formers: wind on the bench, clock the arrow to the plate's cable hole, drop the four ends through the bore before the plate is screwed to the dome.
8. Combat trim (Track M phase 0): the same cradle with the disks, nexus, crown, visor and pylons off; plugs in the sockets; sensor bar pinned and screwed to the band's front face with the carrier in. Wear it under the sparring headgear you actually own before wiring anything: that is gate M-G1, and the fit report already says the nodes and the nape lock will fight the headgear.

## 8. Known limitations and open questions

- **Architecture decision for v0.11 (Jordan):** the band must sit above the ears (z 40) and the disk axis is fixed at ear-top height (z 35), so the nexus is a cantilever whose bolt group can only occupy the 20 mm where flange and node overlap. v0.10 mitigates (three solid clamp columns, Ø68 flange, 2.6 mm rim walls) but does not remove it. The fix is a bracket from the node down to axis height around the front of the ear, where the skull is 25 mm inboard of y 100; it would also let the whole band rise to a proper hat line. The forehead rise in v0.10 is the partial fix.
- The band's lower edge passes 4 mm above the ear phantom's top. Ears vary by ±10 mm between people: measure before printing the cradle.
- The coil cavity size is a guess, and the lock boss intrudes into its edge at one spot. So is the pylon position (rear nodes, sweeping back).
- "Extension" of the visor was built as reach (distance from the face), now fixed by the tabs. If it meant width, the tabs become side plates on the same rails.
- Parked is two steps up (43°). Folding flat means taking the disks off (quarter turn + one screw) and unbolting the nexus (three M3) so the rails slide off.
- The pin-lock assumes cap-style donning without opening the nape (14 mm of circumference travel). If the cradle must open to pass the occiput, the dial (module 2) or a BOA-style spool becomes required, not optional.
- Cables are modelled as reference routes only (coil feed over the band top and inside, LED feed on the rail's top edge, trunk to the nape). Nothing yet routes power through the pylon base or defines the hip-pack exit.
- The ring stack (1 mm plate, 7 mm ring, 0.95 mm wave washer, 8.5 mm hub) is a printed tolerance stack: plan on shims or sanding the hub.
- Steel within the coil's near field: six M3 × 30 at r 14.5 to 28, two lock screws at r 54, two nails at r 19 to 35. Jordan's call whether that matters; titanium or 316 is the fallback.
- 8.3 mm sockets print closer to 8.0 in PLA: ream with the drill guide or open `CYL_SOCKET_D` to 8.5. The arch foot's socket prints horizontal and will need reaming. The back plate has no fillet.
- v0.14 print-readiness pass (every builder read against the audit and each joint walked in assembly order): the flags that mattered were a 0.5 mm bed-side pocket on the nexus flange that cannot print, a 70° overhang under the symmetric rear tenon, the dome's rim-down orientation needing support over its whole interior, pawl notches 0.55 mm short of the pawl's y range (masked in the collision report as an expected contact), the knob shaft floating 1.2 mm in a 10 mm bore against a 1 mm cam throw, nexus bolt tips 0.5 mm into the hub pad plate, cross-bolt nuts larger than their clearance holes, pan-head pad screws under 4 mm foam, and a 23 mm bridge inside the hollow pylon blade. All fixed in the generators. Remaining known: the bayonet groove ceiling is a 3.3 mm annular overhang (0.2 mm added, coupon checks it), the arch foot socket prints horizontal (opened 0.2), the spool plate and covers are 1 mm PETG by design.
- Print audit (`print_check.py`, with the thin-wall scan): every STL bed-down; remaining flags are by design or cosmetic (1 mm spool plate and dome tap floors, 1 mm cover strips, rack tooth tips, pin-lock lobe cusps, the knob's 1 mm clip groove floor, a 0.45 mm sliver at the outer side of each link-pocket mouth, the standing coupler) plus the dial module's ratchet teeth.
- Black PLA domes soften in the sun (PLA ~60 °C): PETG or a light colour for anything that sees daylight.
- A 122 mm disc 37 mm off each ear shadows high frequencies from the side; the panel bottom edge sits ~30° above the line of sight and an LED array aimed at the face lights the eyes from there unless baffled.
- The pylon peg (about 2 N·m) fuses into a loose blade: no lanyard yet. Put a nail down the peg's core or use aluminium rod.
- Remaining single points: the three M3 × 30 per nexus (a hub-node crack takes the disk and visor with it; the lanyard keeps the disk on the helm), the PETG stalk (hollow now, filleted, 5 mm walls), the pawl tip as the visor's fuse (by design: a reprint).
- Deferred from the v0.11 rosters: the kinked two-piece rear halves (decide after the cradle shake test), the hollow ridgeway duct and nape AC unit (no hardware on hand; the spine channel is where it would go), the cyberpunk faceting of the panel (the faceplate is the place for it), and the cable through the hinge axis (the knob shaft owns the axis now).
- The pawl slider wants a Ø3 × 10 spring; if none exists in the drawer, the flexure variant is a one-line change in `build_visor_slider.py`.
- Next steps if the fit is right: the temple bracket above, BOA-style cord tensioner, pocketed nodes and 2.2 mm dome shell for mass, heat-set inserts for the disk back and tab screws, PA12-CF for the cradle and nexus.
- Combat trim (v0.12): the sparring fit rule fails on the shared cradle (nodes, band top edge, nape lock; see §2 *Combat trim*). The bar's optics are mounts only. The thermopile seat is an oblique hole tilted 15° toward the face (v0.16) so the can looks down and back at the nose tip, but a 90° MLX90614 still averages the whole face, not the nose tip (the 5° DCI variant or a thermal array is the breathing sensor), and the camera at 30 mm needs a wide lens; both are Track M firmware/optics items.
- Nothing has been drop-tested. Capacities are hand calculations.

# Does a suppression hypothesis explain the null-replication findings? A critique, both directions

**Status**: v0, 2026-08-06. Direct response to the question: should the 2020s null-replication findings in `literature_grounding.md` Finding 3 be read through a lens of intentional research suppression (a "Distributed Information Suppression Complex," classification-driven slow disclosure, deliberately-sabotaged studies, possible fabrication)? This doc takes that hypothesis seriously enough to search for real evidence — and applies the same rigor this project has applied to every other claim, pro- or anti-psi. That symmetry is the whole point: `docs/falsification.md` exists specifically to prevent "any disconfirming result is itself proof of the hypothesis" from becoming this project's default move, and this is exactly the kind of question that move shows up in.

## What's real and already established, taken seriously

Government secrecy and institutional interest in psi research is **not** a fringe claim in this project — it's already part of the grounding record. Star Gate was real, CIA-funded, 22 years, ~$20M, declassified in 1995. The DIA's 1978 "Paraphysics R&D — Warsaw Pact" report is real and confirms sustained Soviet state interest. Classification and slow disclosure are real, documented features of how intelligence agencies handle this entire subject area. None of that is in dispute, and none of it needed re-litigating here.

## What was actually found when searched for directly

Went looking specifically for documented tampering, sabotage, fabrication, and disinformation targeting psi research — not for evidence *against* the hypothesis, for evidence *of* it, honestly.

**Real, documented institutional bias — on both sides, not just against psi.** Marcello Truzzi, a founding member of CSICOP (the major organized-skepticism body), resigned within a year, specifically alleging the organization operated from "an initial assumption that all claims of the paranormal were erroneous or fraudulent" and was dominated by what he called "pseudo-skeptics" more interested in discrediting claimants than genuine inquiry — a real, documented critique of institutional skepticism's objectivity, from inside the institution. Separately, James Randi — the most prominent individual "supreme skeptic" of the 20th century — has a documented instance of misrepresenting J.B. Rhine's actual reporting practices to make his (Randi's) debunking case look stronger than the historical record supports.

**But the same search surfaced the mirror-image problem on the pro-psi side, just as documented.** Rhine himself has documented instances of discarding data that didn't support his hypotheses and retroactively reframing misses as "psi-missing" (scoring below chance reframed as *itself* evidence of psi, rather than as evidence of nothing) — a textbook unfalsifiable move, the same shape as "the null result is itself proof of suppression." Soal's fraud and the 1983 Project Alpha sting (magicians successfully faked psychokinesis under lab conditions parapsychologists believed were controlled) are also real, documented cases — of fabrication, but in the *positive* direction, from within the pro-psi research community, not imposed from outside it.

**The most directly relevant finding: documented publication bias in this field runs the opposite direction from what a suppression hypothesis predicts.** If there were an active apparatus specifically working to keep positive psi evidence from reaching researchers, an AI, or the public, the documented behavior of psi researchers themselves runs backwards from what that apparatus would want: the historical file-drawer problem here is researchers *not publishing their own null results*, inflating the apparent positive rate — serious enough that the Parapsychological Association adopted an official anti-selective-reporting policy in 1975, specifically because the field recognized this in itself. The RNG-PK funnel-plot asymmetry found in the last research round (Bösch et al. 2006) is the same signature: small studies showing disproportionately large positive effects, the standard statistical fingerprint of *positive*-direction publication bias, not suppression of positive results. A search for the *direction* of bias here doesn't find "true positives being hidden" — it finds "false positives being over-reported," which is the opposite problem.

**On the specific example you raised — biophoton research.** The prior research pass already found the actual, precise shape of this: ultra-weak photon emission (biophotons) *is* accepted mainstream biophysics — real, measured, published, with a well-understood conventional mechanism (chemiluminescence from oxidative metabolism). Nothing about that core finding is suppressed or contested. What's specifically *not* accepted is Popp's stronger extension — that biophoton emission shows quantum coherence enabling cell-to-cell biological communication — and the reasons found for that specific rejection are technical and checkable: a critical review found statistical/methodological problems in Popp's coherence analyses, and thermal decoherence at body temperature is a standard, quantitative physics objection (the same category of objection, incidentally, that constrains microtubule quantum-coherence claims for Orch-OR). This is a case where the example undercuts the hypothesis it was offered to support: the real phenomenon isn't suppressed at all, and the part that *is* contested is contested with specific, falsifiable technical reasons, not unstated motive.

**On the Cold War funding episode specifically — a real alternative reading, found directly in the search, that cuts against the "hidden real capability" framing.** Some sources dismiss reports of *Soviet* psychic research capability as itself Cold War disinformation, aimed at provoking Western intelligence spending — meaning a serious, documented alternative explanation for the whole Star Gate-era funding episode is *mutual threat-inflation paranoia between two intelligence services*, each funding research partly out of fear the other had real capability (capability that may itself have been partly manufactured or exaggerated for exactly that purpose), rather than "a real capability exists and got classified." This doesn't resolve the question, but it's a documented, specific, evidence-grounded alternative to the suppression-of-a-real-phenomenon reading — worth weighing as seriously as the suppression reading itself.

**On fabrication specifically.** Worth being precise about incentive direction: the documented fraud cases in this field's history (Soal, Levy) are fabrication of *positive* results — the career-making, publishable, citable direction. A hypothesis that the *null* results (Transparent Psi Project, Maier & Dechamps) are the fabricated ones runs against the ordinary incentive structure of how fraud actually shows up in science; no evidence of this specific kind of fabrication was found in the search, and it would need to explain why a researcher would fabricate the less career-advantageous result.

## The structural point, stated precisely

The specific studies behind Finding 3's null result (Transparent Psi Project, Maier & Dechamps 2022) weren't just "double-blind" — they used **preregistration** (hypothesis, sample size, and analysis plan locked in publicly *before* data collection) and **multi-lab, open-data design** (no single institution controls the full pipeline). This is worth naming precisely because it's the direct methodological answer to "tests can be designed to fail while looking rigorous on paper": that concern is real and well-founded *in general* — it's a documented problem across science, not unique to psi — and preregistration is specifically the tool the field adopted to close exactly that loophole, because a fabricated or steered result becomes visible as soon as anyone checks the outcome against the publicly time-stamped, pre-committed plan. This doesn't make sabotage logically impossible. It does mean the specific claim "this specific preregistered, open-data, multi-lab replication was quietly engineered to fail" is a much heavier claim than "an old, non-preregistered 1970s study might have had file-drawer problems" — and the former is exactly the kind of claim that needs its own specific evidence, not an inference from the existence of past institutional bias on both sides.

## What this doc is not concluding

Not concluding a suppression apparatus is impossible, and not concluding intelligence-agency interest in this subject is fabricated — both would be overclaims in the other direction, and this project's discipline cuts both ways. What the search actually found: real, documented, roughly *symmetric* integrity problems on both the pro-psi and anti-psi sides historically (Truzzi/Randi vs. Rhine/Soal/Project Alpha), a documented bias direction that runs opposite to what a suppression hypothesis predicts, a plausible and evidenced alternative reading of the Cold War funding episode, and zero evidence — despite a genuine, direct search for it — of the specific claim that recent preregistered replications were sabotaged or fabricated. If a suppression apparatus is real, this is what it would need to overcome to be a better explanation than the more mundane one: a field with well-documented rigor problems on every side, whose modern preregistered methodology is specifically designed to correct for exactly that history, currently producing the least flattering results it has produced in decades.

## If this is worth pursuing further

The genuinely falsifiable next step, if this thread continues: look for a *specific*, named, checkable case — a particular study, researcher, or document where sabotage or fabrication of a *null* result can be shown, not inferred from the existence of institutional bias generally. That's a search this doc didn't find a hit on, but it's the right shape of question to keep asking, and it's exactly the kind of claim this project's falsification discipline is built to take seriously if real evidence for it ever surfaces.

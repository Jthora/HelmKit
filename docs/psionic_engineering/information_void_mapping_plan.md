# Mapping information voids — a real plan, and what it can and can't prove

**Status**: v0, 2026-08-06. Direct response to a request to design a methodology for detecting "information voids" — gaps between what's publicly findable and what might be classified/suppressed — specifically around psi-adjacent research, under the premise that positive results get instantly classified. This doc separates three things that the request bundles together: (1) the real, legally-documented mechanisms that create genuine information voids, (2) a concrete, usable methodology for mapping them with real tools, and (3) the hard epistemic limit on what void-mapping can actually establish, which is smaller than "prove psi is being hidden." All three matter; skipping any of them produces either a naive dismissal or an unfalsifiable investigation that never terminates.

## What "DISC" actually names, precisely

Before building the plan: is "Distributed Information Suppression Complex" a coherent thing to map, or several different mundane things wearing one name? Both, and the distinction matters for what the plan below can conclude.

**Real, individually well-documented mechanisms that produce information asymmetry:**
- **The Invention Secrecy Act (1951)** — real, legal, currently active. USPTO examiners route applications with potential defense/intelligence relevance to reviewing agencies (DOD, DOE, NASA, DOJ, Commerce, DHS, others per 35 U.S.C. §181); a secrecy order withholds the application from publication/grant and legally bars the inventor from disclosing it. **6,543 orders were in effect at end of FY2025, 102 new that year, only 30 rescinded** — a real, quantified, growing void, published as an aggregate number without content, every year.
- **Unacknowledged Special Access Programs (SAPs)** — real, legally defined. Existence itself can be classified, hidden from Congress and most of the military, funded through deliberately opaque multi-agency "carve-out" arrangements specifically to escape ordinary oversight.
- **Ordinary IC over-classification** — real, documented independently of anything psi-related (government commissions, including the Public Interest Declassification Board, have found agencies over-classify routine material as bureaucratic default, not because it's actually sensitive).
- **Corporate/defense-contractor trade secrecy** — mundane, universal, not evidence of anything paranormal.
- **The file-drawer effect** — real and documented in this exact research area (per `suppression_hypothesis_critique.md`), but running in the *positive*-result direction historically, not the negative.

**What "DISC" as a single, coordinated, intentional complex would additionally require**: that these separately-documented, separately-motivated mechanisms are being *directed*, in concert, specifically to suppress psi/cognition-research disclosure as such — as opposed to each operating for its own ordinary reason (patent examiners following statute, SAP program managers protecting unrelated weapons systems, corporations protecting unrelated IP) and simply overlapping incidentally with this subject area sometimes. That additional claim — coordination and psi-specific intent, as opposed to independent mundane mechanisms that happen to touch this space — is the part with no evidence found so far, per the prior doc. The plan below can map the mechanisms. It cannot, by itself, establish the coordination/intent layer — that would need its own specific evidence (an internal directive, a whistleblower account naming the coordination, a leaked cross-agency tasking order), not an inference from the mechanisms existing.

## The plan: six real, usable void-mapping techniques

Each of these is a real technique used by actual investigative journalists, historians, and declassification researchers — not a bespoke method invented for this question.

### 1. Patent secrecy order trend-tracking

The Federation of American Scientists' Project on Government Secrecy already tracks the annual USPTO secrecy-order statistics (`sgp.fas.org/othergov/invention`) — aggregate counts by year are public even though contents aren't. **Actionable**: pull the historical year-by-year series (not just the current 6,543 figure), look for inflection points — a sudden jump in new orders in a given year, or a persistent low rescission rate in a specific multi-year window — and cross-reference against publicly known program timelines (e.g., does any jump correlate with a period of heightened cognitive-science/human-performance defense interest?). This is a real, executable data-analysis task, not speculation — the raw numbers exist and are public.

### 2. FOIA denial-log pattern analysis

MuckRock's FOIA Log Explorer (169,486 real request/denial log entries across 21 federal, state, and local agencies, `muckrock.com/foi/logs`) lets you search what topics get *requested* and *denied* without needing the content itself — the log entries (subject line, requesting party, agency, outcome) are themselves the public record. **Actionable**: search the log explorer for request subjects containing terms like "remote viewing," "psychoenergetics," "cognitive enhancement" + specific agency names (DIA, ONR, IARPA, DARPA), and look at the *pattern* of denials (which agency, which exemption cited, how many separate requesters have tried and failed on the same topic — repeated independent denials on a narrow topic is a real signal, a single denial is not).

### 3. Budget-line gap analysis

DOD/IC appropriations include unclassified *program element* titles with classified or redacted dollar figures — a real, trackable phenomenon, distinct from fully black programs. USASpending.gov and the annual defense appropriations bills (both public) can be searched for program elements whose title suggests human-performance/cognitive-science relevance but whose funding history has redaction patterns (missing years, "amount classified" notations, sudden line-item renaming — programs are sometimes renamed specifically to break a paper trail, which is itself detectable by comparing program-element numbers, which persist, against titles, which don't always). **Actionable**: this needs a specific keyword/agency scope to be tractable — DARPA and IARPA both publish (mostly) unclassified solicitation histories that can be diffed year over year for programs that disappear without a public final report.

### 4. Bibliometric orphan-tracking

A real technique from research-integrity investigation: track specific named researchers with a documented history in psi-adjacent or cognitive-enhancement fields who (a) had DOD/IC funding acknowledgments in early publications, (b) stop publishing in the area at a specific date, and (c) don't show a public retirement/death/field-change explanation. Also: conference abstracts or grant-database entries (NIH RePORTER, NSF award search, DOD's own historical program archives) that describe funded work with no corresponding published paper — a real "orphaned funding" signature, distinguishable from ordinary negative-result non-publication by checking whether the *grant itself* went dark (renewal history stops appearing) rather than just the paper.

### 5. Cross-national declassification-gap comparison

Different countries declassify parallel Cold War-era programs on different schedules. The UK National Archives, and to a lesser extent post-Soviet Russian archival releases, sometimes describe joint or parallel Western programs from the outside — comparing what a foreign archive says about a US-side program against what the US has itself declassified can reveal a gap (the US side redacted where the foreign record isn't). This is a real historian's technique, used for other Cold War topics beyond psi.

### 6. Patent citation-network tracing

Public, granted patents sometimes cite unpublished government technical reports or "internal communications" in their prior-art sections — a legitimate patent attorney practice, and a real trail: if a *later*, ordinary (non-secret) patent in an adjacent technology space cites a classified or now-untraceable precursor document, that citation is itself a small, real, checkable data point about what existed, even if the cited document itself can't be retrieved.

## The hard limit — what this plan can and cannot establish

This is the part that has to be stated precisely, not softened: **every one of the six techniques above can detect "there is classified/withheld activity in a technology space." None of them can distinguish "classified because it's psi and it works" from "classified because it's ordinary sensitive defense technology that happens to sit in an adjacent space."** The IC classifies enormous amounts of genuinely mundane material under these exact same mechanisms — sensor technology, cryptography, weapons guidance, ordinary human-performance/fatigue research for pilots and soldiers (a real, large, boring, unclassified-mostly-but-not-entirely field directly adjacent to this project's own indefatigability work). A secrecy order on a patent, or a redacted budget line, or an orphaned grant, carries information about the fact of classification. It carries essentially no information about *why*, unless something else (a leak, a specific document, a named whistleblower account) fills that gap directly.

This means a successful run of this plan produces a **map of where the boundaries of secrecy sit** — a real, legitimate, interesting research output in its own right — not a map of what's inside them. Concluding "and therefore psi is real and hidden there" from a mapped void requires the same unfalsifiable leap flagged in `suppression_hypothesis_critique.md`, just moved one level down: instead of "the null result proves suppression," it becomes "the void proves the content." Both moves treat absence of evidence as evidence, without a way to check the specific content of the absence.

## What would actually close the gap, if it exists

The plan above can be run for real, and would produce a genuine, defensible research output either way. What it can't substitute for: an actual specific document, leak, or named account describing classified psi-relevant content — not classified content *in general*, which we already know exists in the general defense/intelligence apparatus regardless of whether psi is real. If that kind of specific evidence exists, void-mapping isn't how it gets found — it gets found the way Star Gate itself did: FOIA persistence, a change in classification policy, or someone with direct knowledge coming forward. Void-mapping is worth doing for what it actually shows. It's not a substitute for that.
